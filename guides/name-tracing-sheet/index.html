How to make a name tracing sheet for your child

A name is often the first word a child wants to write. Here is what a useful name sheet looks like, how to use it without turning it into a chore, and how to print one with your child's name on it.

Why start with a name

Many parents start with a child's name because the child already cares about it. It is on their cubby, their drawings and their birthday cake. A word a child wants to write is easier to practice than a word picked for them.

A name also gives a mix of letters in one short word. Some are tall, some sit on the line, some have a tail. That makes it a handy first piece of handwriting practice.

What a good name tracing sheet has

  • A capital first letter, then lowercase. Names are written that way, so the sheet should match what your child will see on a label or a card.
  • A start dot on every letter. Most mix-ups come from starting in the wrong place, so the first dot matters more than the outline.
  • Arrows and numbers for each stroke. Letters like E, k and x have more than one stroke. Numbers show the order and arrows show the direction.
  • Handwriting lines. Lines help a child see where letters sit and how tall they should be.
  • A step from tracing to writing. A sheet that only has traced letters is easy to finish without really learning anything. Look for one that moves from dotted letters to start dots only, with a blank line at the end.

How to use it

  1. Print at 100% (Actual size) so the lines stay the right size.
  2. Sit with your child and trace the first letter together with a finger, following the arrow.
  3. Give them a pencil, a thick crayon or a washable marker. Whatever they find comfortable is fine.
  4. Trace one or two letters, then stop. A few minutes is plenty for a young child.
  5. Praise the try. A wobbly name that starts in the right place is a good result.
  6. Come back to the same sheet another day. Repeating it matters more than doing a lot in one go.

If your child is tired, frustrated or simply not interested, put it away. You can try again tomorrow.

Questions

Should the name be all capitals?

Usually no. Only the first letter is a capital and the rest are lowercase. That is how the name will look in most places.

What if the name has a letter that is hard to write?

Do that letter on its own for a few days first, then add it back into the name. Short, relaxed tries work better than long ones.

Does the name sheet cost anything?

In TinyTracer, the name sheet, the A–Z chart and the capitals sheet are free. Lowercase, numbers and "my letters" sheets are part of Premium.
