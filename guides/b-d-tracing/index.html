Help your child tell b from d

Lots of children write b and d backwards while they are learning. Here is why the two letters are so easy to mix up, and a calm way to practice each one so your child learns where it starts.

Why b and d get mixed up

The two letters are almost the same shape. Each one is a straight line and a round bump. The only difference is which side the bump is on. In the real world, a cup or a chair is still the same object if you turn it around. Letters are different, because turning a b around gives you a d. Children have to learn that rule for letters.

Many children write letters backwards while they are learning to write. It does not mean something is wrong. If you are worried, ask your child's teacher or doctor.

The two letters start in different places

This is the most useful thing to practice. Your hand does a different movement for each letter, and that movement can help it stick.

  • b: start at the top. Draw a straight line down. Then go back up the line a little and draw the bump on the right.
  • d: start with the round part, like a c. Then go to the top and draw a straight line down on the right side.

So b starts with the line and d starts with the round part. If your child starts each letter in the right place, the bump usually ends up on the right side too.

How to practice

  1. Work on one letter at a time. Do b for a few days, then d. Do not mix them on the same page at first.
  2. Trace the letter slowly, following the numbered dot and arrow. Say the start out loud, for example "line down, then bump".
  3. Keep it short. Three or four tries is plenty.
  4. After a few days, try both letters on different days, and then together.
  5. Finish with a word your child likes, such as "bed" or "dad". Real words make the letters feel useful.

Some teachers also use a hand trick. Make two fists with your thumbs up and bring them together. The left hand makes a b shape and the right hand makes a d shape. If it helps your child, use it. If it confuses them, drop it.

Questions

Is it normal for a child to write b and d backwards?

Many young children do while they are learning. If you are worried, ask their teacher or doctor.

Should I correct every backwards letter?

You don't need to. Keep it light. Show where the letter starts and let them try again, and praise the try.

Which letter should I teach first?

Many parents teach one and let it settle before the other. Pick whichever shows up in your child's name or a word they like.

Does TinyTracer fix reversed letters?

We can't promise that. Every letter starts at a numbered dot with an arrow, so b and d start in different places. That gives children something clear to follow.
